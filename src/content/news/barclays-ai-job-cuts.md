Somewhere in a Barclays office today, someone who keeps the bank's systems running is wondering whether their desk is on the list. The British bank said on Thursday it plans to cut about 5,000 jobs in 2026, about 5% of its staff. Behind these Barclays job cuts are real people, and many write software or support the teams that serve customers.

The news came with the bank's third-quarter results. It matters beyond one bank because a major lender is tying cuts, at least partly, to artificial intelligence in its own investor slides. Many companies will be watching how that story is received.

## Where the 5,000 Barclays jobs are going

About 1,000 roles have already gone in the first half of the year. About 4,000 more go by the end of 2026. According to the bank's presentation, about 60% of the cuts fall in support functions, including software developers. Barclays has not said publicly which countries or units are affected.

The slides describe the reasons as "organisational simplification and technology change efficiency driven by AI". Chief financial officer Anna Cross said AI "makes our people more productive" and linked the cuts to simplification and the early effects of AI.

The money side was mixed. Profit before tax rose 12% to £2.3bn, and group income rose 3% to £7.4bn. But profit missed analyst forecasts, as fixed-income trading revenue fell 19%. Barclays also announced a £500m share buyback. Restructuring costs for 2026 are now about £600m, roughly double the earlier plan, and the bank is aiming for more than £1.1bn of savings in 2027.

## The boss calls AI "the icing on the cake"

Here is the tension. On the analyst call, chief executive C.S. Venkatakrishnan played AI down. "It is the icing on the cake, it's not the cake," he said, "because you need to do the infrastructure build to get the benefits of AI." In plain words: years of spending on technology and simpler processes did most of the work; AI is speeding up the last part.

Not everyone accepts that framing. Sharon Graham, general secretary of the Unite union, said Barclays is "using AI as an excuse to discard its loyal and long-suffering workforce". Unite says it is in urgent talks with the bank.

What we don't know yet is how many of the 5,000 are truly down to AI. Barclays gives no split. We also don't know whether more cuts will follow as the 2027 savings target arrives.

Other AI job cuts in 2026 came earlier. Block cut about 4,000 jobs in February, roughly 40% of its staff, and its founder tied that directly to AI. A Morgan Stanley analysis earlier this year said European banks could cut more than 200,000 jobs by 2030, about 10% of staff, with back-office, risk and compliance roles hit most. The same week saw other big companies, such as [Meta](/news/meta-q3-2026-earnings-class-c-shares/), report on how much they are spending on AI.

## If you work in support or software, what to watch

If your job is in operations, support or in-house software at a large company, anywhere in the world, this story is about roles like yours. Those are the functions Barclays and the Morgan Stanley forecast both point to first.

So, will AI replace software developers? The evidence so far says it changes the work more than it ends it. CNBC reported this month that software developer job postings are rising even as AI writes more code. Our read: routine coding and back-office work are most exposed; design, review, security and work with business teams are harder to automate.

Practical steps help. Learn to use AI tools well in your current role. Move towards work that checks, supervises or improves what AI produces. Employment of young workers in AI-exposed fields has fallen, so start early. If you are a Barclays customer, the bank has announced no change to its services.

## Why honesty about AI's role matters more than the number

Our analysis: Barclays deserves some credit. The chief executive did not oversell AI, and the bank was open about where the cuts fall. Streamlining a large bank is a normal business decision.

The harder part is trust. When the slides say "driven by AI" and the boss says AI is only the icing, staff and investors hear two stories. Pairing job cuts with a buyback makes that gap feel wider. The likelier outcome is that more companies will cite AI in cuts, whether it is the main cause or not. Workers deserve a clear account, and fair support while they look for what comes next.

The next sign to watch is the outcome of the Unite talks, and whether 2027 brings a second round of Barclays job cuts.
