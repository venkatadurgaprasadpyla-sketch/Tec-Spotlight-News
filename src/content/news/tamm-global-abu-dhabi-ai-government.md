Think of the last time you renewed a licence. The form, the queue, the second trip because one paper was missing. In Abu Dhabi, a single app called TAMM is built to make most of that disappear, and now the emirate wants to share it with the world.

On 16 October, the Department of Government Enablement – Abu Dhabi (DGE) announced **TAMM Global**, a new company that will license TAMM's technology and know-how to governments abroad. It was set up under the directives of His Highness Sheikh Khaled bin Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan, Crown Prince of Abu Dhabi and Chairman of the Abu Dhabi Executive Council. It is the first time TAMM has been offered for international licensing.

## How TAMM Global will license Abu Dhabi's AI government services

If you are asking what the TAMM app is, here is the short answer. It is Abu Dhabi's one-stop government app, with more than 1,200 public and private-sector services and about 4.7 million users.

TAMM Global will work as an independent private-sector company, partnering directly with governments and institutions. In short:

- It licenses the platform's design, plus the know-how to set it up and run it.
- A government can use TAMM's existing services, move its own services onto it, or order new ones.
- It can be adapted to local languages, laws and institutions.
- The buying government keeps full control of its data, infrastructure, policies and citizen services.

Under its founding law, the company will also hold TAMM's intellectual property, research digital services and train Emirati talent.

## Built over two decades, with AI at the centre

TAMM's roots go back to 2005. In July 2025 it was the global winner of the E-Government category at the UN-backed WSIS Prizes. Its AutoGov feature, unveiled in October 2025 as an "AI public servant", can renew licences, pay bills and book routine health appointments on a user's behalf.

The government's own figures show the effect. According to the launch statement, customer satisfaction reached 94.5%, up from 78% in 2022, before AI was added. It also says the cost of delivering services fell by 30%, and each user saves about nine visits to service centres, around 12 hours, a year.

DGE's chairman, Ahmed Tamim Hisham Al Kuttab, said TAMM was created to improve the quality of life of the community, according to the Abu Dhabi Media Office. TAMM Global, he said, will make capabilities developed and proven in Abu Dhabi available to other governments.

## A new kind of export, with its first partner still to come

In our reading, this is a different model from earlier digital government exports. Estonia and Finland share X-Road, their data-exchange system, as open-source code. India describes its digital public infrastructure as shared designs, not a product. TAMM Global offers a complete, AI-led product.

No foreign customer, contract, price or start date has been named yet. **Rumour:** Axios, citing unnamed sources, reports that the first customer is expected to be in the United States.

## What any government app user can take from this

If you live in Abu Dhabi, the TAMM Abu Dhabi app you use today stays the same. This news is about its reach abroad.

Everywhere else, it shows where AI government services are heading: one app, fewer office visits and an assistant that handles routine tasks before you ask. If your city or country adopts a system like this, two questions are worth asking. Who holds my data? And can I still reach a person when something goes wrong?

## Abu Dhabi is exporting a working system, and fit is the next test

On balance, this is a confident and meaningful step. DGE is not selling a concept; it is offering an Abu Dhabi government app that millions already use, with a UN-backed prize behind it. The promise that each government keeps control of its own data addresses the first worry most countries will have.

It also fits the emirate's wider plan. The Abu Dhabi Government Digital Strategy 2025–2027, worth AED 13 billion (about US$3.54 billion), aims to make Abu Dhabi the world's first fully AI-native government by 2027, meaning AI is built into how services run from the start. It sits alongside other UAE efforts, such as Dubai's [free AI guide for parents](/news/dubai-gendubai-parents-ai/).

There is plenty to watch as it grows. Who signs first, and on what terms? How smoothly does a platform built for one emirate travel to different laws and languages? And as deployments grow, independent reviews could back the satisfaction and savings figures, which so far come from the government itself.

The next proof point arrives soon: Tamm 5.0 is due at GITEX Global in Dubai, 7 to 11 December. Once the first customer signs, the world will learn whether a good government app can feel just as local somewhere else.
