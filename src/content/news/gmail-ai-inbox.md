Monday morning, a full inbox, and somewhere inside a long thread is the one deadline your manager actually cares about. In mid-October, the Gmail AI Inbox started reaching paid Google Workspace accounts, the work and school versions of Gmail.

For now it works only in the US, and only when the account language is set to English (US). Still, in our reading, this is the moment Gemini in Gmail moves from a paid extra for keen individuals into everyday office mail, and that shift is worth understanding wherever you work.

## How the Gmail AI Inbox lists your to-dos

AI Inbox is not a replacement for your normal inbox. It is a separate view in the Gmail menu, on the web and in the mobile app. Google's Workspace Updates post and independent coverage from gHacks and Chrome Unboxed describe two parts.

The first is **suggested to-dos**. The AI pulls action items, deliverables and due dates out of your email threads. The second is **topics to catch up on**, short summaries of busy discussions with many replies.

Rapid Release domains, the companies that take Google's updates early, began a gradual rollout on 12 October. Scheduled Release domains start on 26 October. Google says each phase can take up to 15 days, so some users may not see it until around 10 November.

Google lists Business and Enterprise Standard and Plus, Frontline Plus and similar paid plans. Reports from gHacks and WebProNews say Starter and Education plans follow in the coming months, with no firm date. No extra charge was mentioned. Google also announced Gmail Live, a voice search that lets Workspace users ask their inbox questions on Android and iOS.

Google first showed AI Inbox to testers in January, and by May it was available to US Google AI Ultra subscribers and starting to reach AI Plus and Pro subscribers.

## No switch for IT teams

The detail that IT teams will notice is in Google's own post: there is no admin control for this feature. Neowin also reported this. Admins have no setting to turn off AI Inbox alone.

So how do you turn off Gmail AI Inbox? On a computer, Google's Gmail Help page says to open Settings, choose See all settings, go to the Inbox tab and untick "Show AI Inbox". That hides the view and leaves your normal inbox as it was. Guides from ITPro and HuffPost describe a bigger step: turning off Gmail smart features. That also removes older helpers such as Smart Compose and the Primary, Social and Promotions tabs. On work accounts, your admin's settings may still apply.

Privacy is the other open question. Google says it does not train its Gemini models on your personal emails, and that Gemini in Gmail does not keep your data. Still, the AI has to read your mail to build those to-dos.

## If your Gmail lives outside the US

If you use Gmail at work outside the US, nothing changes yet. The same is true if your account language is not English (US), or if you use free personal Gmail. Google has given no date for other countries or languages.

If you are in the US on a qualifying plan, look for "AI Inbox" in the Gmail menu over the coming weeks. Treat its to-do list as a helpful draft, not a final record. A missed or misread deadline is still your deadline, so open the original email before acting on anything important.

## Helpful for busy inboxes, but control is lagging behind

Our analysis: on balance, this is a sensible feature. Long threads hide tasks, and a short list of to-dos with due dates could save real time for people who live in email. Keeping it as a separate view is a respectful choice.

The weaker part is control. Google has published no accuracy figures, so nobody outside the company knows how often a to-do is wrong or missing. And leaving admins without a switch puts the burden on each user and on IT teams, not on Google. In practice, business tools often gain admin controls after launch, once customers ask. Google has not said whether it will.

The things to watch are when AI Inbox reaches other countries and languages, and whether admins get their own off switch. Those two answers will tell us whether this tool is built around its users, or only around its launch date.
