In 2031, a small capsule is due to fall back to Earth carrying a pinch of soil from a moon nobody has ever touched. That journey began before dawn on Tuesday in southern Japan. Japan's space agency, JAXA, has launched the MMX mission to Phobos, the larger of Mars' two moons.

Japan has done this kind of trip before. Its Hayabusa and Hayabusa2 probes brought asteroid samples home. This time the target is harder and the question is bigger: where did Mars' moons come from? The answer could also hint at how water reached young planets like Earth.

## How the MMX mission left Tanegashima

MMX stands for Martian Moons eXploration. It lifted off on H3 flight No. 10 from Tanegashima Space Center at 04:41 Japan time on 20 October. That was 19:41 UTC on 19 October. JAXA says the rocket flew as planned. The spacecraft separated about 71 minutes after liftoff and entered its planned orbit.

The cruise to Mars takes about ten months. ESA, a partner in the mission, says MMX should arrive next summer, and mission coverage puts that in August 2027. MMX will first settle into a special orbit around Phobos to study it closely, and it will also fly past the smaller moon, Deimos.

Then comes the delicate part. According to DLR, Germany's space agency, a small wheeled rover called Idefix will be dropped onto Phobos around the turn of 2028 and 2029. It is planned to work for about 100 days. Idefix weighs about 25 kg and was built by France's space agency CNES and DLR. It will test the ground and help choose where the main spacecraft lands.

The main spacecraft will then make brief touchdowns at two different sites. Its goal is to collect at least 10 grams of surface soil. JAXA's coring sampler on a robotic arm will dig into the ground. NASA's pneumatic sampler, on a landing leg, will blow puffs of nitrogen gas to push soil into a container. The capsule is due back on Earth in 2031.

NASA also supplied MEGANE, an instrument that measures which elements make up the surface from orbit. NASA will track the spacecraft with its Deep Space Network and keep about 15% of the sample in Houston.

## A small moon with weak gravity and a long road home

Phobos is tiny, and its gravity is very weak. Landing there is less like touching down on a planet and more like docking with a large rock. Every step, from arriving at Mars to two landings and the trip home, carries normal risk.

Exact landing dates are not fixed yet. Mission coverage says the first landing will come no earlier than December 2028, but JAXA's own launch release does not give a date. JAXA has called MMX the first mission planned to orbit Phobos, sample its surface and make a round trip to the Mars system.

## Why a Phobos sample return could matter to all of us

So, how did Mars get its moons? Scientists have two leading ideas. Phobos and Deimos may be asteroids that Mars captured. Or they may have formed from debris thrown into space when something huge struck Mars long ago.

Each answer would be valuable. If the moons are captured asteroids, they may be the kind of rock that carried water and organic material to the inner planets, Earth included. If they are impact debris, the soil could hold tiny pieces of ancient Mars, reaching labs without anyone landing on Mars.

For students today, the timing is worth noting. The soil arrives in 2031, so some of the people who study it may still be in school now. Anyone can follow the Japan Mars moon mission on JAXA's MMX site and NASA's mission page.

## Our reading: a patient bet that is already paying off

This is our analysis, not a fact from the sources. The JAXA H3 launch matters on its own. The rocket failed on its first flight in 2023, and now it has sent a spacecraft towards another planet. That helps rebuild trust in Japan's main launcher.

The honest caution is time. Five years is a long road, and the samples may not settle the origin question cleanly. On balance, MMX looks like [careful, shared science](/news/category/science/). Japan leads, with help from NASA, CNES, DLR and ESA, and with parts built by private firms.

The next milestone to watch is the arrival at Mars next summer. After that, the story moves to a small moon with weak gravity, and to at least ten grams of soil that could answer an old question.
