A week ago, Tesla showed off a sports car it says can fly. On Wednesday it showed something quieter: where the money really goes. The Tesla Q3 2026 earnings show sales standing still, profit falling sharply, and spending pouring into robots and AI.

For the three months from July to September, revenue was flat at US$28.05 billion. Net profit fell 43% to US$787 million. In the same report, Tesla said its Optimus humanoid robot has entered production. The numbers tell us Tesla is now spending like a robotics and AI company, while still earning like a carmaker.

## Tesla Q3 2026 earnings: flat car sales, heavy research spending

The results come from Tesla's quarterly update, filed with the US Securities and Exchange Commission on 21 October. Revenue a year earlier was US$28.10 billion, so sales barely moved. Money from selling cars fell 3% to US$20.6 billion. Energy storage revenue fell 16% to about US$2.9 billion. Services and other income grew 33% to US$4.6 billion.

Costs tell the bigger story. Research and development spending rose 69% to US$2.76 billion. Total operating expenses rose 39% to US$4.78 billion. As a result, operating profit fell 56% to US$718 million. That means Tesla kept less than 3 cents of every dollar of sales as operating profit, down from almost 6 cents a year ago.

Tesla gave three reasons for the drop: higher research spending on AI, Optimus and the Cybercab; less money from selling regulatory credits to other carmakers; and thinner margins in energy. It delivered 486,500 cars in the quarter, 2% fewer than a year before. It still holds US$45.4 billion in cash and investments, and generated US$1.5 billion in free cash flow, down 62%.

## Robots are on the line; the payoff is not

Two product updates stood out. Tesla says Optimus production has started on "high-volume lines" in Fremont, California, which used to build the Model S and Model X. It calls these its first production-quality robots. Its Cybercab, a car with no steering wheel, has carried Robotaxi riders in Austin, Texas, since 3 September.

On the call, Elon Musk was careful about speed. He said Optimus output will grow slowly at first before it speeds up. Tesla suggested outside buyers may wait until around the end of 2027.

Tesla also told analysts it now expects to spend about US$20 billion on factories and equipment in 2026, down from "more than US$25 billion". Tesla said on the call that spending will rise again in 2027 and reach "tens of billions" in 2028. Tesla did not say which projects get less.

Merger questions were not taken on the call. A day earlier, on SpaceX's own call, Musk said combining Tesla and SpaceX could be "a good idea", and that both boards had set up committees of independent directors. He said it is not a certainty.

## Why is Tesla stock falling after a "beat"?

Many readers own US shares through apps from India, the UAE, the UK and elsewhere, so this one is worth a line. Adjusted profit per share, which leaves out some costs such as stock-based pay, was US$0.48. That beat analyst forecasts. Revenue was about in line. But the full, official (GAAP) profit per share was US$0.22, down from US$0.39. The shares fell about 3% in late trading. This is not investment advice; it is a reminder to check both numbers.

If you are thinking about a Tesla car, the picture is steady rather than exciting. Deliveries and car revenue are flat, and the new money is going elsewhere. The Robotaxi and Optimus work is real, but for now it is limited to the United States and early stages.

## Our reading: a long bet, paid for by a car business that has stopped growing

There is real good here. Starting robot production on real factory lines is a meaningful step. A driverless ride service in Austin is a working product, not a slide. And US$45 billion in cash gives Tesla room to be patient.

The harder truth is that the core business is not growing, and costs are. Excitement around [the new Tesla Roadster](/news/new-tesla-roadster/) does not change that; the Roadster is not due until 2028. On balance, the likelier path is a few more quarters of thin profit while Tesla waits for robots and robotaxis to earn their keep. The bet may well pay off. It is simply not paying yet.

The next update to watch is whether Optimus output grows quarter by quarter, and whether the SpaceX talks turn into a real proposal.
