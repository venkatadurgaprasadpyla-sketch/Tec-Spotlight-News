### Privacy claims, in Amazon's own words (quote exactly)

From A (launch post):
- "Bee is private—audio is processed in real time then deleted, and customers have control of their data."
- "No audio is ever stored. Audio is processed into insights in real time and deleted after processing."
- "Transcripts are encrypted using keys that only your device creates and controls. Transcripts are accessible only to you unless you choose to share them."
- Bee Private Compute "processes your conversations inside a secure and isolated processing environment where no one, including Amazon, can access your data."
- "As with all Bee devices, audio is never stored, cannot be accessed or shared, and users are prompted to gain permission from call participants each time a recording is started."
- "You can delete your transcripts and summaries at any time."

From B (Bee Private Compute post, 23 Sep 2026):
- "Only you hold the keys to your information. Amazon has no master key that can unlock your content."
- Built on the AWS Nitro System and NitroTPM. Amazon links a "third-party audit" by security firm **Trail of Bits** (PDF hosted on Amazon's own site) and a technical paper on amazon.science. I could not find the audit on Trail of Bits' own public reports list, so describe it as "Amazon says it was reviewed by Trail of Bits".
- Bee now uses Amazon account sign-in for new customers; Amazon says this "does not give Amazon access to your private Bee data".

From C/D (Bee site): "Audio is processed instantly and then deleted." and "Customer-owned keys never leave the device".

**Check before writing:** The Bee site lists "On-device processing" as a feature, but Amazon's own posts say conversations are processed in a secure cloud environment (Bee Private Compute on AWS). Do not say processing happens only on the device. Safe wording: the audio is turned into text and then deleted, and the processing happens in a locked-down Amazon cloud system that Amazon says even it cannot read.

## Open question

Bee's promise is that audio disappears and only you can read the notes. But the new Amazon.com link turns things you say into shopping suggestions. How much of what you say feeds Amazon's shopping recommendations, and can an outside expert confirm the "even Amazon can't see it" claim? Also open: when Eclipse ships, and whether either device will be sold outside the US. And how will the people around you (who never agreed to a microphone) feel about it? Phone-call consent rules also differ by country and US state.

## Reader angle (worldwide)

- For now this is a US product on Amazon.com in US dollars. Readers elsewhere can't simply buy it locally, and the AI features (40+ languages) may still work if they import one, but that is not confirmed by Amazon.
- The bigger point for everyone: "AI that listens to your day" is becoming a cheap, mass-market product (US$75), from the company that also runs the biggest online shop. The real cost is the US$11.99 a month for the useful features.
- Practical tips the writer can offer: tell people when it's on (the light is small on a wrist), check local laws on recording calls before using Eclipse, keep the Amazon.com link switched off if you don't want shopping suggestions, and delete old transcripts.
- Who it may help: forgetful people, students in lectures, carers tracking care plans (Amazon names lectures and care plans as templates, A). Bee's site carries user reviews from people with hearing loss and ADHD (E), but these are Bee's chosen testimonials.
