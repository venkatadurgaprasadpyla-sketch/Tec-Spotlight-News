## Quotes (verbatim)

1. C.C. Wei, CEO, earnings call (via Reuters, D):
   > "Looking ahead, we continue to observe consumer and price-sensitive end-markets segments are being challenged due to the impact of rising component prices and macroeconomic uncertainties. As such, we are being prudent in our business planning going into 2027."
2. C.C. Wei on Texas (via Reuters, D):
   > "Texas is one," ... "We sent teams to Texas to do all the evaluation."
3. Wendell Huang, CFO (TSMC release, A):
   > "Our business in the third quarter was supported by strong demand for our leading-edge process technologies."
   > "Moving into fourth quarter 2026, we expect our business to be supported by continued strong demand for our leading-edge process technologies, including the steep ramp-up of our 2-nanometer technology."
4. Gary Tan, portfolio manager, Allspring Global Investments (via Reuters, D):
   > "The key risk is that TSMC's caution translates into broader earnings downgrades across a value chain accustomed to sustained upward revisions, but for now, this looks like sensible expectation management rather than a change in the sector's long-term fundamentals."
5. Sravan Kundojjala, SemiAnalysis (via CNBC, E):
   > "Even after those checks, TSMC says demand remains well above its supply capacity."

Reuters also reports TSMC called demand "extremely robust" with no sign of a slowdown in the "AI mega trend" (D) - those are short phrases, not full sentences; use them as reported phrases.

## Rumours and things to label

- **Rumour / not confirmed:** a TSMC factory in Texas. Reuters reported on 30 Sep 2026 (sources) that TSMC is evaluating a Texas investment (https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/tsmc-evaluates-potential-texas-investment-sources-say-2026-09-30/). On the call Wei confirmed only that Texas is being **evaluated** ("Texas is one"; teams sent). No decision, site or amount is confirmed. Write it as "TSMC is studying Texas", never "TSMC will build in Texas".
- Terafab: talks only, no deal.
- Small mismatch: CNBC converts revenue to US$46.36 billion; TSMC's own US$ figure is **US$46.72 billion**. Use TSMC's.
- Context note: Reuters ties the sell-off to worries about further US interest-rate hikes; candidates.md mentioned hot US inflation data. Do not state a cause beyond what Reuters says.

## The open question

Is the 2027 caution about AI or about everything else? TSMC says AI demand still exceeds supply, but phones, PCs and other price-sensitive products are being squeezed by rising component (memory) prices and an uncertain economy. The question for 2027: can AI keep growing fast enough to carry TSMC while consumer devices slow, and will the "prudent" planning ripple down to suppliers as Allspring's Gary Tan warns?

## Reader angle (worldwide)

- TSMC makes the chips inside most AI services and high-end phones (Nvidia and Apple are key customers, per Reuters). Its results are the clearest single reading of whether the AI boom is still real: on this evidence, yes.
- The caution matters to ordinary buyers: Wei names rising component prices as a strain on consumer devices. That hints phones and laptops may cost more or sell less in 2027 (an inference: say so carefully).
- Smartphones fell from 30% to 21% of TSMC revenue in a year: the world's top chipmaker is now mainly an AI-and-data-centre company.
- For investors worldwide (TSMC is on Taiwan and NYSE), results beat forecasts, yet shares fell with the wider chip sell-off, a reminder that good news is already priced in.
- Supply-chain geography: 79% of revenue from North American customers; second Arizona plant from Q3 2027.
