## Verified facts (each with source)

1. **Headline number:** up to **30 ± 6 mm** of global mean sea-level rise from Thwaites by 2100 (abstract). In the results, the 5 ± 1 km grounding-zone runs give **30.1 ± 5.7 mm**. 30 mm is about 1.2 inches (CNN and Gizmodo use 1.2 in). [Nature abstract and Results](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-77976-1)
2. **"About double" earlier estimates. What "earlier" means:** the comparison is with the **ISMIP6** ensemble (the international Ice Sheet Model Intercomparison Project for CMIP6), run under the **same constant-forcing scenario**. ISMIP6 mean for Thwaites: **15.2 ± 21.8 mm** (very wide spread; some ISMIP6 runs even slow down late in the century). The paper says 30.1 mm "is twice the mean of the ISMIP6 ensemble under a constant climate forcing scenario". It is not a comparison with IPCC totals or with all past Thwaites studies. [Nature, Results](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-77976-1)
3. **Range across settings (paper):** narrow 1 ± 1 km grounding zone gives 14.3 ± 3.1 mm (about the ISMIP6 mean); 3 ± 1 km (best fit of the calibration) gives a "lower-bound" 19.4 ± 5.2 mm, which is 130% more than... wording in paper: "an increase of 130% relative to the mean of the ISMIP6 ensemble"; 5 ± 1 km (matching recent satellite observations) gives 30.1 ± 5.7 mm. Simply adding a grounding zone raises sea-level rise by 51% versus traditional grounding-line models; each extra 1 km of grounding zone changes the projection by about 22% on average. [Nature, Results](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-77976-1)
   - Writer note: the "130%" figure reads oddly in the paper (19.4 vs 15.2 is about +28%). Do **not** use the 130% number.
4. **Scenario assumption (important):** the projections use a **constant climate forcing baseline**, built from average conditions in **1996–2023**, which the authors call a "lower-bound scenario". That is what "even without additional climate warming" means. They assume no year-to-year variability in ocean heat to 2100. [Nature, Introduction/Results/Methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-77976-1)
5. **Warming sensitivity test:** if future warming raised ocean temperatures by 0.5–0.7 °C, basal melt would rise by about 10 m per year and projected ice loss by about 15%. The authors conclude the result depends more on grounding-zone width than on moderate warming. [Nature, Results](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-77976-1)
6. **2038 and the ridge:** the abstract says grounding-zone processes enable the glacier's "unpinning as early as 2038". In the results: with 4–6 km grounding zones there is about a **60% probability** of crossing (unpinning from) the **Mouginot Ridge by 2100**; "at 5–6 km, unpinning occurs as early as 2038", letting the grounding line reach the next high point, **South Ridge**, well before 2100. So 2038 is the earliest case in the widest-zone runs, not the most likely date. [Nature abstract and Results](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-77976-1); 60% also in [UCI](https://ps.uci.edu/news/uc-irvine-nasa-jpl-melting-of-antarcticas-thwaites-glacier-worse-than-thought/) and [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/10/15/climate/thwaites-melt-study-doomsday-glacier)
7. **The science idea:** most models treat the line where ice leaves the seabed and starts to float (the "grounding line") as a sharp boundary. Satellite radar shows a "grounding zone", kilometres wide, where seawater pushes in under grounded ice with the tides and melts it. The model only reproduced nearly three decades of observed retreat, thinning and speed-up when grounding zones of 2–5 km were included. [Nature abstract](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-77976-1)
8. **Calibration:** checked against observations from the 1990s to now (the paper's hindcast starts in 1996). Runs with no grounding zone were four times less likely to match the historical record than runs with a zone a few km wide. Note: the UCI release phrases this as change "four times more pronounced than traditional models"; use the paper's wording. [Nature, Results](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-77976-1), [UCI](https://ps.uci.edu/news/uc-irvine-nasa-jpl-melting-of-antarcticas-thwaites-glacier-worse-than-thought/)
9. **Data:** synthetic aperture radar from Finland's ICEYE satellite constellation detected km-scale shifts in the grounding zone along Thwaites' main trunk; seawater gets several km under grounded ice and lifts it with the tides. [UCI](https://ps.uci.edu/news/uc-irvine-nasa-jpl-melting-of-antarcticas-thwaites-glacier-worse-than-thought/)
10. **Context:** Thwaites sits in the Amundsen Sea Embayment, which accounts for roughly one third of Antarctic Ice Sheet mass loss. [UCI](https://ps.uci.edu/news/uc-irvine-nasa-jpl-melting-of-antarcticas-thwaites-glacier-worse-than-thought/)
11. **Background (from CNN, not the paper):** Thwaites is nicknamed the "Doomsday Glacier", is roughly the size of Florida, currently contributes around 4% of global sea-level rise, holds enough ice to raise seas by more than 2 feet (about 65 cm) if it collapsed, and could ultimately lead to about 10 feet (about 3 m) because it holds back neighbouring ice. Gizmodo's caption: about 120 km (80 miles) wide. Attribute these to CNN / Gizmodo if used. [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/10/15/climate/thwaites-melt-study-doomsday-glacier), [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/ocean-water-is-seeping-under-the-doomsday-glacier-and-its-sea-level-threat-just-doubled-2000825851)
12. **Next step:** Rignot told CNN the team plans to travel to Thwaites in 2028 to put instruments at the grounding line that can record seawater flushing in and out. [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/10/15/climate/thwaites-melt-study-doomsday-glacier)

## What the study does NOT claim (from the paper's own limits)

- It does **not** say Thwaites will collapse by 2100, and 30 mm is **Thwaites only**, not total sea-level rise.
- It does **not** say unpinning will happen in 2038; that is the earliest case, in the widest grounding-zone runs. Overall about 60% chance by 2100 for 4–6 km zones.
- It is **not** a future-warming scenario. Climate is held at the 1996–2023 average ("lower-bound"); a warmer ocean adds about 15% in their test, and they did not model a possible rise of warm water higher in the ocean (thermocline shoaling).
- Limits the authors list: a fixed grounding-zone length (in reality it varies); a fixed ice front (no calving); uncertain starting shape and seabed depth; ocean and cavity feedbacks not included; basal friction held constant and no subglacial water network, which "may lead to an underestimation" of thinning and speed-up; not a fully coupled ice-ocean model. [Nature, Discussion and Methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-77976-1)
- The paper says its results "lie toward the upper end of previously reported uncertainty ranges", which is a softer framing than "worse than thought". [Nature, Discussion](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-77976-1)

## The open question

Is the grounding zone really 4 to 6 km wide along most of Thwaites, and does it stay that way? The result swings about 22% for every kilometre, and nobody has yet measured the zone directly from below. The 2028 field trip is meant to answer that. Until then, the 30 mm figure is a well-calibrated model result, not an observation.

- 30 mm (about 1.2 in) sounds small, but it is from one glacier, on top of other sources, and higher baseline seas make storm surges and high-tide floods reach further. Dubai/Abu Dhabi, the Netherlands, Pacific islands) will feel it through flood planning, insurance and infrastructure costs.
- Even with no more warming, the model says the rise continues: "damage that has already been done" (Rignot). Cutting emissions still limits what comes after.
- Tech angle for our site: the advance came from better physics plus commercial satellite radar (ICEYE SAR) and Bayesian calibration against 25+ years of observations.
