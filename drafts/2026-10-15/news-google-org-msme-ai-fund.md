If you run a small shop, a tiffin service or a two-person design studio, you know the hard part isn't always the work. It's the paperwork, the loan forms and finding the next customer. A new $15 million fund is aimed squarely at those problems.

## What happened

On 14 October, Google.org, Google's charity arm, announced $15 million in funding for The/Nudge, an Indian organisation that works on livelihoods. The money will go into building AI tools for micro, small and medium businesses (often called MSMEs).

The tools are meant to help with three things: getting formal credit (bank loans instead of expensive informal lenders), finding new markets, and keeping up with compliance (the paperwork and filings the government requires).

The programme has two parts. First, an open call for proposals, where innovators can pitch their ideas. It's run through a site called Samriddhi by The/Nudge, which says the challenges open in January 2027. For now, you can register early interest there. Second, a voice-led platform in several languages that teaches work skills to people in smaller towns (often called Tier 3 and Tier 4 towns) and then matches them with small businesses that are hiring.

According to the announcement, India has more than 90 million small businesses, employing over 345 million people.

## Why people are talking about it

Subhashree Dutta, CEO of the^delta, which is building the programme with Google.org, called it "one of the largest philanthropic commitments to India's MSMEs".

What we don't know yet: which tools will be built, when they'll be ready, and whether they'll be free to use. Those answers depend on which proposals get picked. The announcement does mention "open standards" and "open digital tools", which suggests some of what's built may be free for anyone to use.

## What this means for you

- **In India:** if you run a small business, nothing changes today. But it's worth watching for free tools that help with loan applications, compliance paperwork or selling online. If you're a developer or startup with an idea for small businesses, the open call on the Samriddhi site is a real chance to get funded. The challenges open in January 2027, but you can register early interest on the site now. If you live in a smaller town, the training platform could lead to paid work with local businesses.
- **In the UAE:** the fund is aimed at Indian small businesses. If you're an Indian founder or developer living in the UAE, check the Samriddhi page to see who can apply, because the reports we read don't say. And if you help run a family business back home, keep an eye on The/Nudge for any tools that launch, so you can pass them on.
