### What generation makes up the 1,100 MW? (open question)

- **Not disclosed.** No result (release copies, trade or local press) names the fuel or technology behind the 1,100 MW. The release says only "generation resources". NiSource says more detail comes on 28 Oct.
- Context (do NOT state as the Microsoft mix): GenCo's already-approved build is gas-heavy. IURC approved GenCo's proposed resources in June 2026, including combined-cycle gas turbines and battery storage (NiSource release "Regulatory approvals underscore strength..."; IURC filings in cause 46362 describe two 1,300 MW combined-cycle gas units at Schahfer and a 400 MW battery at the former Mitchell station). Energy-Storage.News also noted fossil fuels form a major part of the Amazon package. Safe wording: "NiSource has not said what kind of plants will make up the 1,100 MW; the generation GenCo already has approval to build for data centres is mainly gas, plus batteries."
- Single-sourced: one promotional piece claims the deal shows a move to renewables, with no detail. Ignore.

### Regulator (IURC) step

- **Not confirmed** whether the Microsoft contract itself needs IURC approval. The release's forward-looking-statement section lists "receipt, timing and terms of required regulatory approvals" as a risk, which implies some approvals are needed, but none is named. Mark as an open question.
- Precedent: the Amazon special contract and power purchase agreement went to the IURC (approved 17 June 2026, per NiSource release); the Alphabet contract was listed as "pending IURC approval" in NiSource's 10-Q. FERC approval of the Amazon PPA was also noted as a separate step (single-sourced from filing summary).
- Same week: IURC approved a framework (reported 14 to 16 Oct 2026) letting NIPSCO and its affiliate GenCo buy spare capacity from each other and setting the pricing, over objections from Indiana's Office of Utility Consumer Counselor (OUCC) and an industrial customer group. Actual trades still need separate IURC approval through NIPSCO's Resource Adequacy Tracker. Critics worry GenCo is not rate-regulated in the same way. Sources: IBJ, Inside Indiana Business, Daily Journal (all one syndicated report, so count as one independent source); official cross-check not found. Mark as **news-reported**.
- GenCo itself: IURC approved NiSource's GenCo strategy earlier (NiSource and NIPSCO news pages "NiSource Achieves IURC Regulatory Approval for GenCo Strategy"). Circle of Blue calls it America's first data-centre-only utility. LaPorte County had objected to the GenCo spin-off (one news summary, single-sourced).

### Prior hyperscaler deals with the same utility

- April 2026: NiSource signed a long-term supply deal with an Alphabet subsidiary for a northern Indiana data centre (site not named; supply from summer 2026, rising to 300 MW from 2030 per 10-Q) and expanded its Amazon agreement. Sources: Reuters via StreetInsider/Yahoo, DCD, NiSource 10-Q. Confirmed.
- Amazon: NIPSCO to supply about 3 GW to Amazon data centres in northern Indiana (Utility Dive); 15-year deal; NIPSCO says about US$1bn of savings for customers (Capital B Gary). Citizens Action Coalition argues savings should be larger (about US$3.5bn) if Amazon paid industrial transmission rates (Capital B Gary, single-sourced, advocacy view).
- So Microsoft is the third hyperscaler with this utility. Confirmed by multiple reports.

### The La Porte campus

- Announced June 2024 by Indiana Gov. Eric Holcomb: US$1bn, 245,000 sq ft, 489 acres in Radius Industrial Park, up to 200 jobs by end of 2032 (IEDC official release + City of La Porte + Inside Indiana Business). Confirmed.
- 2026: expanded plans (17 buildings; first phase six data centres plus substation; DCD). Groundbreaking 17 June 2026 with Microsoft president Brad Smith; first data centres due 2029 (DCD, NWI Times). Smith's "more than 600 permanent jobs" claim is single-sourced via search summary; avoid or label.

### Microsoft's "pay our way" pledge (context)

- 13 Jan 2026: Brad Smith announced "Community-First AI Infrastructure": Microsoft says it will work with utilities and regulators so its data centres pay their full power costs and households do not absorb them; also water, taxes, jobs. Sources: Network World, The Hill, POWER Magazine, Data Center Knowledge. Official Microsoft On the Issues blog not opened (seen only via reports). Confirmed by several independents.
- Other Microsoft power deal: Chevron/Engine No. 1 gas power for a West Texas campus (about 2.67 GW, first power targeted 2028; DCD, Data Center Knowledge, EnergyNow June 2026). Useful contrast: Microsoft is increasingly leaning on gas for AI.

### Wider context: AI power demand and who pays

- IEA "Energy and AI" (April 2025): data centres used about 415 TWh in 2024 (about 1.5% of world electricity); base case about 945 TWh by 2030 (just under 3%); US and China nearly 80% of growth. Official IEA pages + Scientific American, APPA. Confirmed.
- Scale: 1 GW is often compared to the demand of roughly 800,000 to 1 million US homes (Congressional Research Service R48646: 100 to 1,000 MW = 80,000 to 800,000 homes; Consumer Reports: 1 GW about 1 million homes). Suggest "roughly 800,000 to 1 million American homes" with CRS as the official source.
- Large-load tariffs: about 23 to 24 US states had approved at least one tariff making big new customers pay their own grid costs by mid-2026 (law-firm/EEI summaries; numbers vary). Pennsylvania PUC adopted a large-load tariff framework on 30 Apr 2026 (official PUC release). Confirmed in outline; treat counts as approximate.
- Evidence on bills is mixed: Rutgers NJ State Policy Lab found no large detectable rise in local residential bills from data centres in 2014 to 2024, but warns big new projects could change that. PJM's market monitor reported added costs passed to ratepayers from data-centre load (single-sourced figure via secondary summary; avoid exact number).
- Local: NIPSCO bills rose sharply 2023 to 2025 (Capital B Gary, WSBT); NIPSCO blames grid upgrades, not data centres.
- Related big deal: Google signed a 3.59 GW deal with Constellation (early Oct 2026): 890 MW of new nuclear uprate capacity over 20 years plus 2,700 MW over 15 years, first power 2028 (Transformer Magazine, Yahoo/Zacks, Slashdot/Ars). Confirmed.

## Open questions for the judgment section

1. What fuel will the 1,100 MW be? If gas, how does that square with Microsoft's carbon-negative-by-2030 goal? (Undisclosed; 28 Oct call.)
2. Does the contract still need IURC sign-off, and will consumer advocates (OUCC, Citizens Action Coalition) challenge the savings maths?
3. The savings are projections over 20 years. What happens if AI demand cools or a hyperscaler leaves early? (Simply Wall St flags financing/cost-recovery risk for NiSource.)
4. Good side: a clear, public "the big customer pays" model that other states are copying.

## Reader angle

Everywhere AI grows, someone must build power plants. This deal is a test case for a model where the tech company pays for the plants and lines, so household bills are protected. Readers can watch for "large-load tariffs" or similar rules where they live.
