Citation: Science 394, 311–315 (2026), DOI 10.1126/science.aee0658.
   - **To check before writing:** the Science abstract mentions Greenland and Antarctica, while MIT/WHOI describe Antarctica; confirm the scope and the exact cliff count. Caveats: few very tall cliffs in the data; rates cover months, not decades (Earth.com).

2. **TOP PICK: A brain enzyme may help memory stay strong in old age: boosting the gene Pla2g2f protected memory in ageing mice, and it was most active in people whose brains had Alzheimer's-type plaques but no memory loss** (science; health; Nature Neuroscience, 15 Oct)
   - Summary: researchers (co-author Amar Sahay, Massachusetts General Hospital) found that Pla2g2f, which makes a secreted phospholipase (a fat-breaking enzyme), rises in the hippocampus with age and inflammation. Lowering it made brain ageing and memory worse in mice; raising it protected against many changes, and raising it in middle-aged mice changed the course of later memory loss. Human brain tissue from deceased donors showed the highest activity in "resilient" people. Paper: "An aging-sensitive secreted phospholipase confers neuroprotection and cognitive resilience".
   - Why it matters: everyone worries about memory in later life, and "resilience" is a hopeful new angle on dementia research. Caveats to state clearly: mostly mouse work; the human evidence is an association in a small set of brains (one report says 13); no treatment yet. An independent expert (Timothy Hohman, Vanderbilt) commented in Nature's news story.
   - Official/journal: https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03303-9 (Nature news, 15 Oct; find the Nature Neuroscience paper DOI and any MGH release)
