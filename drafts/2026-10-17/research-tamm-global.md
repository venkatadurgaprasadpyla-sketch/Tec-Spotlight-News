## Headline (plain words)

Abu Dhabi sets up TAMM Global to offer its AI government-services app to other countries.

## What happened (2–3 lines)

On 16 Oct 2026 the Abu Dhabi Department of Government Enablement (DGE) announced a new company, TAMM Global. It acted on the directives of His Highness Sheikh Khaled bin Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan, Crown Prince of Abu Dhabi and Chairman of the Abu Dhabi Executive Council. The company will license the technology and know-how behind TAMM, the emirate's one-stop government services platform, to governments and institutions abroad. Countries that adopt it keep control of their own citizens' data.

## Why it matters to readers

- It is the first time a Gulf government is offering its own citizen-services "super app" to other countries as a product. Before this, the UAE adopted other countries' ideas; now it is offering its own.
- For readers in any country, it shows the direction public services are moving in: one app, AI that renews licences and pays bills before you ask, and fewer trips to an office.
- For UAE residents, nothing changes in the app they use. The news is about TAMM's reach abroad.

## TAMM's track record at home (background)

- **Roots:** Khaleej Times traces TAMM to a 2005 physical service centre in Al Dhafra, which became a digital platform after the 2006 e-government strategy. Al Kuttab's statement also refers to 2005. A glossary site says 2019, and earlier reports describe TAMM as a 2018–19 app relaunch. **Safest wording: "TAMM's roots go back to 2005."** Sources: Khaleej Times; Arabian Business / launch statement.
- **Growth:** about 750 services in 2023 (Gulf News); 800+ at TAMM 3.0 (GITEX 2024, Media Office); about 1,100 to 1,150 in 2025 (WSIS / Fortune); 1,200+ now. Users: 3.3M (mid-2025) → 4.5M registered (H1 2026) → 4.7M (Oct 2026).
- **H1 2026:** about 55 million transactions, about 98% completed digitally, about 77% processed instantly (Gulf News, 15 Aug 2026, citing DGE; the headline says 55.5M). [SINGLE outlet, DGE data]
- **Awards:** global winner of the E-Government category at the UN-backed WSIS Prizes 2025 (Geneva, 7 July 2025). [2+ incl. official] Sources: DGE news page; Zawya / TahawulTech; Time Out Abu Dhabi; TAMM on X.
- **AutoGov (TAMM 4.0, GITEX Oct 2025):** billed as the "world's first AI public servant". It can renew licences, pay bills and book routine health appointments on its own. [2+] Sources: Fortune; The National; Counterpoint.
- **Next:** Tamm 5.0 is due at GITEX Global in Dubai, 7–11 Dec 2026 (The National 7 Oct; tbreak). No features published yet.
- **Bigger plan:** Abu Dhabi Government Digital Strategy 2025–2027, AED 13 billion (about US$3.54 billion). It aims to be the world's first fully AI-native government by 2027, digitise 100% of government processes and roll out 200+ AI solutions. In Mar 2025 it signed a multi-year partnership with Microsoft and Core42 (G42). [2+ incl. official] Sources: Media Office; DGE (Microsoft/G42 news page); Arabian Business; CIO.com.
- Fortune (14 Oct 2026) and TheNextWeb raise trust and privacy as the main challenges. That is useful balance for the judgment section.

## Comparable government-tech exports (for context)

- **Estonia (X-Road):** Estonia and Finland set up NIIS in 2017 to look after the X-Road data-exchange code, which they released as open source (MIT licence). Finland runs it as Palveluväylä; in 2018 the two countries set up the first cross-border X-Road link. Ukraine's Trembita is built on the X-Road model with Estonian help. Independent sources put X-Road in **20–25+ countries**. e-Estonia's own claim of "130 countries" covers e-governance solutions in general, so avoid that number. Sources: x-road.global (NIIS, official); e-estonia.com; Nortal; GovTech Intelligence Hub.
- **India (digital public infrastructure):** India says it has MoUs or agreements on India Stack / DPI with **24 countries** (Feb 2026; PIB, official). The government frames this as sharing designs and principles, "not export of a product". That is a useful contrast with TAMM Global's licensing model. UPI is live in about 8–11 foreign countries (counts differ: PIB says "over eight", a later factsheet reportedly says 11), including the UAE, Singapore, France, Nepal, Bhutan, Sri Lanka and Mauritius. MOSIP (open-source digital ID) country counts differ by source, so do not cite them. Sources: PIB (official); IBEF; PSU Connect.
- **Singapore GovTech:** shares its experience through an international collaboration programme and the Digital Government Exchange rather than selling a branded app (tech.gov.sg).
- **Angle for the writer:** Estonia and India mostly share open designs or code. TAMM Global is a company licensing a complete, AI-led product with its intellectual property. That makes it a different and newer model, and it explains why the first customer matters so much.

## What to watch (constructive framing for the judgment section)

1. Who signs first, and on what terms. No buyer, price or date has been named yet. The US interest is reported by Axios only.
2. How well a platform built for one emirate travels to places with different laws, languages and systems. The company says it can be adapted to each.
3. How the promised data control works in practice for each buyer.
4. Tamm 5.0 at GITEX Dubai, 7–11 Dec 2026. It is the next proof point at home.
5. Whether satisfaction and savings figures, which so far are the government's own, are backed by independent reviews as deployments grow.
