## 1. Verified facts

### A. What Tesla's own pages say (checked 16 Oct 2026, about 05:15 UTC)

From tesla.com/roadster [1]. These are short exact quotes; the writer should summarise them, not copy them.

- Three versions:
  - **Performance**, "By Reservation": "Road ready with seating for two." Active aerodynamics and a "multi-mode suction system" for downforce.
  - **Thrust**, "Limited Release": "Race ready with seating for two." Adds cold-gas thrusters at the rear that move the car "from 0-60 in under 1 second".
  - **Rocket**, "Invite Only": "Air ready with seating for one." Thrusters all around the car for forward, upward and turning movement. "So yes, it can fly." The page adds: "**Note:** Not approved for public road use."
- Footnote 1: flight-related features are "Available with the Rocket trim only, which is not available in all regions."
- Footnote: the features and specs on the page reflect the Thrust option, which is "an optional upgrade".
- Fan system: Tesla says the suction can hold the car upside down "even when stationary". Track Mode lowers the car, with high-downforce and low-drag modes.
- Other page claims: steer-by-wire with a yoke; magnesium, titanium and carbon fibre to save weight; "Ano White" paint and "Gold Glazed" tinted glass styled after space gear; the line "We already put Roadster in a rocket. Now we put a rocket in Roadster." It calls the car "the quickest vehicle in the world" in its page description.
- **Not on the official page:** top speed, range, horsepower, battery size, weight, price, production date. Only "0-60 in under 1 second" (Thrust) appears as a number.

From tesla.com/roadster/reserve [2] (default selection is Performance):

From the Roadster Reservation Agreement (linked on the reserve page) [3]:
- A reservation only holds your "approximate priority". It is not an order.
- Any estimated price "is only an estimate". Tesla says it cannot confirm the exact price yet.
- Tesla "cannot guarantee when your vehicle will actually be delivered or if it will be delivered."
- Either side can cancel before the buyer configures the car, with a full refund of the reservation payment.
- Tesla does not keep the money separately or in escrow, and pays no interest. Disputes go to individual arbitration, with a 30-day opt-out.

### B. Deposit conflict: settled

The reports all agree once you add the two payments together:
- **Performance:** US$5,000 today + US$45,000 within 10 days = **US$50,000 in total.** Confirmed on Tesla's page [2], and matches Edmunds [4], MotorTrend [6] and Electrek [9].
- **Thrust:** US$5,000 today + US$245,000 within 10 days = **US$250,000 in total.** Reported by Edmunds [4], Digital Trends [11] and a Reddit r/teslamotors screenshot [12]. One X post (The Cybertruck Guy) adds "one per customer" [13], which is unconfirmed. Not checked on Tesla's page myself, so attribute it: "Edmunds and others report...".
- **Rocket:** invite only; no amount published.
- So "US$50,000 vs US$5,000" and "US$250,000 vs US$5,000" are not conflicts. US$5,000 is the first payment and US$50,000 / US$250,000 are the totals within 10 days.
- **No car price has been announced** for any version [4][7][9]. MotorTrend guesses it will cost more than the 2017 figures; label that as their view [6].

### C. The event: what the press saw (their observations, not Tesla's claims)

- Time and place: Thursday 15 Oct 2026, scheduled for 7:55 p.m. local time (8:55 p.m. ET, about 00:55 UTC on 16 Oct), at SpaceX's test site in McGregor, Texas, near Waco [5]. Livestreamed on Tesla's X account [14] (not opened; X is not scrapable here).
- **0–60 mph in 0.81 seconds:** the timer shown in the demo (Road & Track [7], MotorTrend [6], InsideEVs [8]; Edmunds says it was "shown in a video" [4]). MotorTrend notes it does not know the tyres, the surface, or whether the timing included "rollout" (a common drag-strip allowance) [6]. So: "shown in Tesla's demo", **not** an independently measured figure.
- **Flying demo:** the car drove up a tall ramp and went upside down using fan suction, then left the ramp, spun with gas jets and landed. In another stunt it jumped over parked Teslas (a Semi, Model 3 and Model Y) and flipped in mid-air. MotorTrend estimated the ramp at "at least 5- or 6-stories" [6][7].
- **Nobody was inside the flying car.** It was remote-controlled (Edmunds [4]; MotorTrend also calls it remote control [6]). Edmunds says it **bounced when it landed**, as if it had no suspension [4].
- **Hardware seen** (MotorTrend's count and Tesla's displays): 10 cold-gas thruster nozzles (4 at the back, 4 underneath pointing down, 2 at the nose for turning). Pressure tanks hold mostly nitrogen at up to 10,000 psi [6]. Musk said on X that the SpaceX package has "~10 small rocket thrusters" [5]. Musk also said the car can refill the air tank itself [7]. That is a claim.

### E. Street legal?

- Tesla's own page says the **Rocket** version is "Not approved for public road use" [1].
- Asked about it, Musk said he was "not necessarily sure it's legal in all jurisdictions" and "Depends on your street", while saying it will be a real car someone can own (InsideEVs [8]).
- Electrek: the stunts in the headlines use the limited or invite-only versions, which are not road legal [9].
- No word from US air or road safety regulators (FAA, NHTSA) in any source I checked. One Instagram reel mentioned an FAA no-fly zone over the site; **unconfirmed**, do not use.

### F. History (2017 to 2026)

- **16 Nov 2017:** Tesla showed a Roadster prototype as a surprise at its Semi truck event in Los Angeles. Musk promised 0–60 mph in 1.9 s, a 200 kWh battery, 620 miles of range and seats for four. It was expected in 2020. Deposit: US$50,000. Founders Series: 1,000 cars, US$250,000 paid up front (CNBC, 2017 [10]). Top speed "above 250 mph" and a US$200,000 base price come from later reports (Electrek [9][15]); CNBC 2017 did not list them.
- **2020 and later:** every target date was missed (CNBC [5]; Electrek keeps a list of delays [15]).
- **Then vs now:** the 2017 car was a 2+2 four-seater. The new car seats two (Rocket: one) [9]. The 2017 claim of 1.9 s is now beaten on paper by every version. The 620-mile range has not been repeated with any confidence (see the range row above).
- Deposits: Tesla is again asking US$50,000 for a standard reservation, the same as in 2017 [9]. One 2017 Founders buyer told CNBC his US$250,000 was in effect "an interest-free loan" to Tesla [5]. Tesla has not said how many 2017 deposit holders are still waiting, or how they will be treated. **Unknown.**
- Side note (fact): a first-generation Roadster was launched into space on a SpaceX Falcon Heavy in 2018. Tesla's page refers to it ("We already put Roadster in a rocket"). Background only.

### G. Business context

- CNBC: the Roadster is unlikely to have a material financial effect on Tesla [5]. CNBC also says Tesla reports Q3 results after the market closes "on Wednesday" (likely 21 Oct; confirm before using) [5].

## 4. Cautions for the writer

1. **Tesla's claims vs what the press saw.** Only "0-60 in under 1 second" (Thrust), "it can fly" (Rocket) and "Not approved for public road use" (Rocket) are on Tesla's page. The 0.81 s, over 230 mph, 1,500+ hp and 2028 figures are stage or demo claims: write "Tesla says" or "Musk said".
2. **Do not state a range for the new car.** 620 miles was the 2017 promise; this week's reports conflict (Edmunds 620, Electrek "over 300", R&T "not discussed").
3. **The flying car had nobody inside.** It was remote-controlled, and Edmunds saw it bounce on landing. Do not write that "a driver flew" it or that it is a "flying car you can buy".
4. **Deposits are not prices.** US$50,000 (Performance) and US$250,000 (Thrust, reported) are refundable reservation totals, paid as US$5,000 now and the rest within 10 days. There is no price for any version. Show both currencies only as US$.
5. **2028 is production, not delivery,** and it rests on Musk's word. Mention the 2020 promise calmly, as fact, without mocking. The reservation terms themselves say delivery is not guaranteed [3]; that is a fair, neutral point for readers.
6. **Street legal:** the Rocket is officially not approved for public roads. Musk's "Depends on your street" is a quote; do not turn it into a promise either way.
7. **Rumours:** "one per customer" for Thrust and an FAA no-fly zone are unconfirmed. Leave them out or label them **Rumour:**.
8. **Reader angle (worldwide):** Tesla's site lists Roadster pages for many countries (including India and the UAE), but the Rocket is "not available in all regions". Do not say it is on sale in any particular country beyond a reservation page existing.
9. No hype words ("insane", "mind-blowing"). Outlet headlines use them, so do not echo them. Do not copy sentences from Tesla's page; summarise.
