### Apps and games (compatibility)
- **Nvidia's claim (attribute it):** Justin Walker, Nvidia senior director of products, told The Verge: "All your apps, all your games, everything's going to run great on Spark." (The Verge, Sean Hollister, 15 Oct 2026: https://www.theverge.com/tech/1011705/nvidia-rtx-spark-everything-runs-on-arm)
- Earlier, at Computex, Jensen Huang promised RTX Spark would run essentially every Windows app (reported by Windows Central: https://windowscentral.com/hardware/laptops/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-promises-new-rtx-spark-windows-on-arm-chips-will-run-every-windows-app-ever-made). Treat as a company promise, not a fact.
- **How it works:** Most games and older apps are written for Intel/AMD (x86) chips. On RTX Spark they run through **Microsoft's Prism**, a translator built into Windows on Arm. Translation costs some speed, mostly on the CPU side. (Tom's Hardware; Engadget, links below.)
- **What testers found:** Windows Central says most everyday apps are already Arm-native and that Prism has been tuned so translated apps "feel just like native ones"; most mainstream anti-cheat now works on Windows on Arm; some games still need workarounds (Red Dead Redemption 2 had to be switched from Vulkan to DirectX 12 to stop crashing). Tom's Hardware saw translation overhead hold back the CPU-heavy Gears of War: E-Day.
- **Anti-cheat:** Nvidia says it is working with the major anti-cheat makers (Engadget). Windows Central's separate gaming piece still tells PC gamers not to pre-order one mainly for games: https://www.windowscentral.com/hardware/surface/surface-laptop-ultra-gaming-pc-rtx-spark
- Engadget: games supported at launch include Control Resonant, Gears of War: E-Day, NBA 2K27 and World of Warcraft: Forever; Adobe is rebuilding Photoshop and Premiere for the platform.

### Battery life
- No independent battery-life hours yet. Tom's Hardware's early test excluded battery results. Windows Central and Engadget both found performance on battery about the same as plugged in; Windows Central says the graphics chip "drains power fast" when pushed hard, but everyday use should last a workday.
- Nvidia claims it is twice as efficient as an Intel Core Ultra 9 285H + RTX 5070 laptop when gaming at 60 watts (**Nvidia's claim**, reported by Engadget; not independently tested).

## 5. Reader angle (buyers worldwide)

- **Who it is for:** people who run AI models on their own machine (privacy, no monthly AI fees), video editors and 3D creators who want one thin laptop. Nvidia's pitch: "No subscription. No per-prompt fees." (Nvidia product page.)
- **Who should wait:** gamers who play competitive online games (anti-cheat is still being rolled out), anyone on a normal budget, and anyone outside the US/UK, where prices and dates are not yet confirmed.
- **Money reality:** the cheapest model is about US$2,600. Tom's Hardware's point: that money buys a lot of cloud AI use.
- **Practical tip:** before buying, check that your must-have apps and games (especially ones with anti-cheat, older drivers, printers, VPNs) run on Windows on Arm; most mainstream apps do, but not every one.
- **Bigger picture:** a third serious chip choice for Windows (after Intel/AMD and Qualcomm), which could push prices and battery life forward for everyone.

## 6. Fair judgment points (good and bad)

Good:
- Independent tests back the local-AI claims: faster than Apple's top laptop chip in at least one AI-agent test.
- Same speed on battery as plugged in (two outlets).
- Huge shared memory (up to 128 GB) lets big AI models fit on a laptop; cheaper than a similar-memory MacBook Pro.
- Broad maker support: Microsoft, HP, Dell, ASUS, Lenovo, MSI on day one or close to it.

Bad / open questions:
- High prices; most buyers are better served by ordinary laptops (Windows Central, Engadget).
- Gaming works but is not its strength; translation overhead and anti-cheat gaps remain.
- CPU single-core speed trails rivals.
- No independent battery-life hours yet.
- Availability outside the US/UK is unclear; MSI and Lenovo Yoga Pro 9n prices and dates not confirmed.
- "Everything runs" is Nvidia's promise; real-world proof will come over weeks of use.
