2. **A 75,000-year-old Neanderthal was probably killed by a wooden spear, after being cared for through an earlier face injury** (science; promoted from the 20:10 UTC next candidates)
   - University of Cambridge team (Dr Emma Pomeroy; excavations led by Prof Graeme Barker), published 15 Oct in *Scientific Reports*. The Neanderthal "Shanidar A" from Shanidar Cave, Iraqi Kurdistan, was about 45 when it died. A 15 mm round hole through the left shoulder blade, bevelled like an exit wound, points to a sharpened wooden spear entering the chest from the front, angled downwards; the team calls it possibly the earliest evidence of a wooden spear attack on a human relative. A split front tooth with tartar on the exposed root suggests the person lived at least a year after a heavy blow to the face, likely with help (for example soft food). Caveats from the university: the context is unknown, a hunting accident is possible, and involvement of modern humans cannot be ruled out; ABC quotes an outside expert saying more evidence is needed.
