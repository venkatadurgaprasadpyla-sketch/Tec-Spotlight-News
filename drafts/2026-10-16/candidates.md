2. **A 75,000-year-old Neanderthal was probably killed by a wooden spear, after being cared for through an earlier face injury** (science; promoted from the 20:10 UTC next candidates)
   - University of Cambridge team (Dr Emma Pomeroy; excavations led by Prof Graeme Barker), published 15 Oct in *Scientific Reports*. The Neanderthal "Shanidar A" from Shanidar Cave, Iraqi Kurdistan, was about 45 when it died. A 15 mm round hole through the left shoulder blade, bevelled like an exit wound, points to a sharpened wooden spear entering the chest from the front, angled downwards; the team calls it possibly the earliest evidence of a wooden spear attack on a human relative. A split front tooth with tartar on the exposed root suggests the person lived at least a year after a heavy blow to the face, likely with help (for example soft food). Caveats from the university: the context is unknown, a hunting accident is possible, and involvement of modern humans cannot be ruled out; ABC quotes an outside expert saying more evidence is needed.

2. **The world votes to stop adding leap seconds: from 20 May 2027, official world time (UTC) runs continuously** (science; metrology, with a big tech angle). Confirmed (official resolution published).
   - Why: changes how every clock, phone, computer network and satellite system keeps time; HN front page (#11, 119 points); covered by Scientific American, CNET, timeanddate, The Conversation; easy to explain to any reader worldwide.
   - Facts (official, BIPM Resolution 5 of the 28th CGPM, 2026): continuous UTC takes effect on **20 May 2027**; the allowed gap between atomic time (UTC) and Earth-rotation time (UT1) rises from under 1 second to a maximum of **3,600 seconds (1 hour)**, enough "for several centuries". Reasons given: critical infrastructure (transport, energy, telecoms, space) needs time without jumps; a 2025 expert workshop put the chance of a first-ever **negative** leap second at about 30% by 2035, which has never been tried and is seen as high risk, with preparation costs compared to the millennium bug; big tech firms smear leap seconds in different ways, causing inconsistent time. The official text is the French version.
   - Date and place: voted 15 Oct 2026 at the CGPM in Paris (timeanddate; Scientific American says the vote was "this morning"). Exact vote count not given; one Facebook post says "Friday", which conflicts, so use 15 Oct from timeanddate and confirm with a BIPM press release if one exists.
   - Caveats/open question: future generations must decide what to do once the gap nears one hour (a possible "leap hour" far in the future); radio time signals may need until 2035–2040 to adapt (ITU Resolution 655); astronomers and navigators who need Earth-rotation time will rely on published UT1–UTC corrections.
   - Official: https://www.bipm.org/en/cgpm-2026/resolution-5 (DOI https://www.doi.org/10.59161/CGPM2026RES5E)
   - Independent: https://www.timeanddate.com/news/astronomy/no-more-leap-seconds-utc-2026 ; https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/scientists-vote-to-end-leap-seconds-redefining-how-the-world-keeps-time/ ; https://www.cnet.com/science/world-timekeepers-kill-the-leap-second/ ; https://www.cnn.com/2026/10/13/science/leap-second-elimination-time-standard (background, before the vote)
