## Corrections to the brief

1. **Wording.** Not "withdrawing" or "postponed". Official wording: Solidigm has decided to **halt / discontinue its review** of a **pre-IPO round and an IPO**. Newsroom headline: "Solidigm to Discontinue Review of Potential IPO, Reaffirming Commitment to Long-Term Growth and Shareholder Value". No IPO was ever formally filed or priced, so there was nothing to "withdraw". Use "halted its review" / "dropped plans for now". Avoid "cancelled for good" (the company leaves room for other options).
2. **Size.** Reuters (Sept 2026, via US News/Investing.com) reported the IPO could **raise about US$15bn** at a valuation of **up to US$150bn**, as early as 2027 (Nasdaq discussed). Bloomberg called it a potential **US$10bn** listing. Write "US$10bn–15bn, according to Reuters and Bloomberg reports". The company never confirmed any size.
3. **Shares.** Fell as much as ~3% early, then **closed up about 4.4% at 1,277,000 won** (Seoul Economic Daily: +4.42%; AJP: +4.4%; CNBC: "over 4%"). Not "3–4%". (TradingKey says +4.74%; outlier, ignore.) KOSPI closed +0.30% at 5,550.30 after an intraday fall of more than 4%.
4. **US plant is not Indiana.** The US fab Solidigm needs money for is a **possible first US NAND fab, with upstate New York the lead candidate** (Reuters via Investing.com, JoongAng Daily, DigiTimes, Sept 2026). No deal, site, size or date confirmed; SK hynix said "no specific plans have been confirmed". **Indiana (West Lafayette)** is a different project: SK hynix's own ~US$3.87bn–4bn **HBM packaging plant**, groundbreaking 27 Aug 2026. Do not mix them.
5. **Solidigm acquisition dates.** Deal signed Oct 2020 (~US$9bn). Phase 1 closed 29 Dec 2021 (Intel SEC filings), when Solidigm was formed. Phase 2 (final) closed **27 Mar 2025**, ~US$1.9bn (Intel 8-K/10-K). So "bought 2020-21" should read "agreed in 2020, completed in two steps in 2021 and 2025".

## Reader angle

- **PC and phone buyers:** short term, nothing changes; SSD prices are driven by the AI-led NAND squeeze, not by this IPO. Longer term, less fresh money for new NAND factories could mean supply stays tight, keeping SSD prices high. (Analysis, label as such.)
- **Investors (incl. retail via SKHY ADR or KOSPI):** shows Korea now protects parent-company shareholders from "double listing" dilution; markets cheered.
- **Supply chain:** Solidigm makes all its NAND in China; a US fab would diversify supply. That plan is now slower or needs another funding route.
- Judgment: protecting small shareholders is a good thing; the cost is slower funding of new capacity during a shortage.
