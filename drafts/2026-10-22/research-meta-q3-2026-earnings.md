## Teen mental-health legal settlement — be precise
CONFIRMED (O2 + I8/I1/I9):
- Q3 costs include a US$9.89 billion charge (rounded US$9.9B) tied to the "August 2026 Multistate Attorneys General Agreement".
- Meta's release describes it as an agreement with 52 attorneys general to resolve litigation over teen mental health and social media, providing for payments of **up to about US$18.59 billion** (O2). Media in August reported "about US$18 billion" (I9).
- Payments run over about 10 years to the states, for youth well-being (mental-health programmes, digital literacy, phone-free school zones, grants to schools/local governments) (I9, I11).
- The case: filed 2023 by California AG Rob Bonta and a bipartisan coalition of states; alleges Meta designed features that drove compulsive use by young people and misled the public about risks (I9, I10). Meta denies the claims; the settlement is not an admission of liability (O5/O2 language, I-sources).
- Teen product changes under the deal (Facebook + Instagram, under-18s): default 2-hour daily limit that a parent must approve turning off; apps blocked midnight–6 a.m. by default; most notifications muted overnight and in school hours (DMs/some safety alerts exempt) (I9, I10). Two independent outlets; not checked against the court document — write as "under the agreement, as reported".
- Still needs court approval (I9) — say "awaiting court approval" only with attribution.

UNCONFIRMED / handle carefully:
- Total figure varies: US$18.59B (Meta release) vs about US$17.1–17.2B (some reports of court filing). Use Meta's "up to about US$18.59B" and attribute it.
- About US$5.3B is conditional on TikTok and YouTube adopting similar measures; if they do, daily limit drops to 1 hour — reported by I9 / Android Authority; not in official source found. Write as "reported" or drop.
- Party count conflict (52 AGs per Meta vs "47 states and territories" or "50 states + DC" in some reports). Use Meta's "52 attorneys general".
- Why the charge (US$9.89B) is smaller than the "up to" total is NOT explained. Do not guess (analysis only: likely accounting for the probable/estimable portion — label as our reading or leave out).
- School-district cases are a SEPARATE track (federal multidistrict litigation): Meta settled the first school-district bellwether in May 2026; next trial set for Feb 2027 (O5 10-Q). Don't merge with the AG deal.

## New non-voting Class C shares — CONFIRMED (O3/O4 + I2/I4/I5)
- On 19 Oct 2026 the board declared a one-time stock dividend: one new non-voting Class C share for every Class A and Class B share held (in effect a 2-for-1 split) (O3, I4, I12).
- Approved by written consent of entities tied to Mark Zuckerberg and Priscilla Chan, so there is no general shareholder vote (O4, I5).
- Charter change takes effect 19 Jan 2027 (O4 per I5, I2). Record and payment dates still to be set by the board (O3) — say "early 2027; exact dates to be set".
- Zuckerberg controls about 60.6% of voting power with about 13.4% economic stake (O4, I2). (56.1% = the consenting entities as a group, I5 — different measure; use 60.6%.)
- Purpose (Meta): let the company use Class C shares for employee pay, acquisitions and raising capital (including for AI build-out) without diluting Zuckerberg's voting control (O4, I2, I4).

## Analysis (label as analysis in the story)
- Business is strong (ads up, prices up), but costs are climbing faster: legal bills from teen harm and AI spending made free cash flow negative this quarter.
- Class C shares let Meta raise money and pay staff in stock without touching who controls the company; shareholders who don't like that have no vote on it. Governance critics will see this as concentrating power further.
- For the settlement: the money is large in absolute terms but is about one-sixth of one quarter's revenue (TheWrap headline frames it as "a blip") — the teen-feature changes may matter more to families than the dollar figure.
